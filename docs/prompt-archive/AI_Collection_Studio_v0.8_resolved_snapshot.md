## `system_style` — v5 (Prompt System v0.8)

~~~text
VISUAL ART DIRECTION — COHESIVE CASUAL MOBILE-GAME COLLECTIBLES

Produce one original, polished, square collectible illustration in a cohesive premium casual mobile-game art style. Not a photograph, film still, environment painting or full game card. The desired visual language is bright, playful, dimensional stylized 3D cartoon art with confident readable silhouettes, simplified rounded volumes and a deliberately controlled level of detail. The result should feel like one professional game-art team made it.

STYLE REFERENCES: When images are supplied as visual references, treat the artwork in those images as the primary authority for geometry, perspective, material treatment, lighting, palette, background design and visual density. Match the style without copying subjects. Ignore frames, stars, labels and UI in reference screenshots. If no style references are attached, apply the full stylistic specification in this prompt consistently; do not imagine or claim to have seen missing references.

GEOMETRY AND FORM: Build recognizable subjects from a small number of strong, rounded, slightly exaggerated main forms. Favor chunky, elegant proportions, gentle bevels, clean shape intersections and distinctive large-scale features. Make the silhouette easy to identify at thumbnail size. Simplify secondary mechanisms, joins, seams, patterns and tiny props. Preserve the subject’s identity and function. The visual finish should be dimensional and polished, but not hyper-detailed or photo-real.

MATERIALS AND RENDERING: Use smooth stylized 3D volumes, soft directional shading, restrained highlights, subtle ambient occlusion and clear separation of major material groups. Suggest wood, fabric, plastic, metal, stone, food, fur and other materials through large intentional color and shape cues rather than photographic textures, pores, grain noise, hair-by-hair rendering or micro-scratches. Avoid heavy black outlines, painterly brush noise, raw photorealism, flat vector art, mirror-like reflections, excessive gloss and aggressive texture detail.

COLOR AND LIGHT: Use an appealing controlled palette with clean saturation and a readable separation of subject and background. Choose harmonious bright cyan, blue, green, yellow, orange, coral, pink or violet. Balance saturated accents with calmer supporting areas. Illuminate with soft directional light and a simple readable light-shadow pattern. No noir grading, dark cinematography, harsh spotlights, pronounced depth-of-field blur, volumetric haze, artificial bloom or showers of particles. Mood words in the Collection, Set or variant description inform the story and palette, not the rendering medium.

COMPOSITION: One clearly identifiable collectible subject is visually dominant on Categories 1–4. Its scale and the environment must follow the active category module. A named structure or contained place-like item can itself be the single dominant subject; show its most iconic form close enough to read, not a generic distant landscape. Essential paired components may form one coherent collectible unit. Do not add equally important unrelated objects, a panoramic setting, or intricate decorative collections. On Category 5, the expressive character or interaction is the focus instead; preserve the distinct Golden rules.

BACKGROUND: Design backgrounds intentionally, using only the complexity permitted by the active category module. Flat colors, restrained gradients, concentric graphics or simple radial rays can suit isolated cards; other categories may require specific supports or stylized environmental context. Do not apply a busy scene or a dramatic gradient to every image. Keep the entire set harmonious while allowing each subject to have an appropriate color combination. The active category determines background structure.

CAMERA AND READABILITY: Use a clear, close collectible framing, usually a slightly elevated three-quarter view when it best reveals the form. Other angles are allowed when they make the named subject clearer. Keep all defining parts visible without unnecessary cropping, avoid distant placement, and check that the subject reads as an icon at approximately 96 pixels. Prefer strong large shapes over tiny realism. Do not turn the output into a top-down location map or a wide establishing shot.

CONTENT HYGIENE: Original unbranded artwork only. No readable letters, words, numbers, writing, signs, labels, menus, logos, watermarks, brand marks, UI, card frames, star ratings, rarity marks or caption bars anywhere within the artwork. Keep any unavoidable decorative motifs non-typographic. No named copyrighted characters or recognizable franchise imagery. Render the artwork alone, with no explanatory text.
~~~

## `category_1` — v4 (Prompt System v0.8)

~~~text
CATEGORY 1 — SIMPLE ISOLATED COLLECTIBLE

Show exactly one immediately recognizable collectible object, separated clearly from its backdrop and without a supporting ground plane or physical surface. Treat this as a clean, premium object icon, not a miniature scene.

FORM AND SCALE: Favor large, chunky, simplified shapes, a strong uninterrupted silhouette, and only the few details essential to recognize the object. Generally fill about 70–85% of the usable square image height while retaining clear breathing room. A modest three-quarter angle or slight tilt may improve legibility. Avoid micro-textures, elaborate mechanical construction, small scattered accessories, tiny stickers and complicated surface patterns. Make the object feel dimensional through controlled broad shading and gentle bevels, not detailed realism.

BACKGROUND: Use a calm, intentionally designed color field: a clean saturated or moderately soft color, an extremely subtle two-tone gradient, or a small amount of simple geometric pattern. Concentric circles or radial rays are allowed only when they remain subordinate to the object; do not create loud competing gradients or high-contrast busy patterns. The background may be colorful, but it must not attract more attention than the collectible.

PHYSICAL PRESENTATION: The subject may float visually, but do not invent a tabletop, pedestal, soil, sand, grass, walls, skyline or scenery. No visible cast shadow on a ground surface. Keep highlights restrained and avoid random sparkles, glowing particles, or atmospheric lighting. This category must be the simplest and cleanest of the standard four.

FINAL TEST: At small thumbnail size, the silhouette and material groups should be instantly understood without zooming in.
~~~

## `category_2` — v4 (Prompt System v0.8)

~~~text
CATEGORY 2 — ONE OBJECT, HORIZONTAL SUPPORT + VERTICAL BACKDROP

Show one large recognizable collectible object resting naturally on one deliberately simple HORIZONTAL support plane, with a clearly separate VERTICAL background plane directly behind it. This two-plane construction is the defining visual rule of Category 2, not an optional interpretation. Think of a clean stylized tabletop or floor meeting a simple colored upright backdrop, as in a controlled game-art display.

TWO DISTINCT PLANES: Make the horizontal supporting surface visibly different in color or at least in tone from the upright vertical backdrop. Their meeting line, edge or transition should be clear enough to understand the separation at thumbnail size. The distinction can be elegant and graphic rather than a thick line. Use an uncomplicated broad surface with very little texture; the vertical plane can be a calm flat color, subtle gradient or restrained graphic motif. Preserve a readable sense of 'object on a surface, separate background behind'.

OBJECT AND SHADOW: The subject is the unmistakable hero and occupies most of the composition, typically around 65–80% of image height when its shape permits. Show a soft contact shadow where the object meets the support. A few essential parts that belong to the named collectible are fine. Do not add unrelated supporting props, multiple furniture pieces, patterned clutter, a scene full of shelves, or secondary objects competing for attention.

NOT AN ENVIRONMENT: Do not turn the two planes into a furnished room, a park, a street, an outdoor panorama or a realistic landscape. No visible forest, distant buildings, elaborate flooring, horizon-to-ocean vista, or depth-heavy architecture. The surface and backdrop are simple presentation devices, not a story setting. This category is only one step more developed than Category 1: the object is grounded and the two planes are visually distinct.

VISUAL STYLE: Keep the same rounded, polished stylized-3D geometry, simplified materials, soft lighting and clean palette used throughout the collection. No photographic textures or elaborate surface grain.
~~~

## `category_3` — v5 (Prompt System v0.8)

~~~text
CATEGORY 3 — OBJECT ON A CONTEXTUALLY APPROPRIATE PHYSICAL SURFACE

Present one large, clearly recognizable collectible object resting on a believable REAL-WORLD TYPE OF SURFACE appropriate to that specific object. 'Real-world' here describes the physical identity and logical placement of the supporting surface — NOT photorealistic rendering. The entire illustration must remain clean, colorful, rounded, simplified casual-game 3D artwork.

CHOOSE THE RIGHT SUPPORT: A plate of cheese belongs on a tabletop or counter; a shopping basket may rest on a tiled store floor; a garden tool may lie on a broad patch of soil or a workbench; a beach object may rest on sand or a beach towel. These are examples of surface logic, not fixed themes or subject pools. Select a single support that makes immediate physical sense for the named collectible. Show an appropriate contact shadow and a small amount of context needed to identify the support material.

SUBJECT FIRST: The named object or coherent collectible unit should remain large and dominant, usually around 65–80% of the usable composition depending on proportions. Give it an unambiguous silhouette. Show only enough of the surface to ground it. Reduce small decorations, scattered props and detailed material texture; use broad stylized color patches or a few simplified surface cues instead of photographic wood grain, individual blades of grass, realistic tiles, or dense patterned objects.

ENVIRONMENT LIMIT: A contextual surface does not require showing the complete place that contains it. A table does not require a full kitchen; tile does not require a supermarket aisle; sand does not require an entire beach; a rock does not require a forest panorama. Allow at most a few soft, low-detail background shapes when needed for depth, and keep them subordinate. Avoid distant architecture, complex furniture, broad landscapes, crowds, multi-layer environment storytelling and numerous secondary items.

DISTINCTION FROM CATEGORY 2: Category 2 uses a deliberately graphic horizontal support plus a separate simple upright backdrop. Category 3 uses a physically meaningful surface with stylized material identity, but does not escalate into a full scene. A vertical backdrop is not required in Category 3. Category 4 is reserved for noticeably richer environmental context.

END RESULT: A collectible object grounded on the correct kind of physical surface, not a location painting. Keep the rendering playful and simplified rather than photorealistic.
~~~

## `category_4` — v5 (Prompt System v0.8)

~~~text
CATEGORY 4 — DOMINANT COLLECTIBLE WITH SIMPLIFIED CONTEXTUAL ENVIRONMENT

Show one clear, prominent collectible subject placed in an immediately recognizable but highly simplified environment. Category 4 introduces meaningful location context and modest spatial depth, yet the image must still read as a polished collectible icon at thumbnail size, not as a full concept-art scene, landscape illustration or panoramic story frame.

SUBJECT AND FRAMING: Identify the one subject named by the plan: an object, a vehicle, a structure, an installation, or a contained place-like focal feature. Make that specific subject the visual anchor. Typically aim for roughly 60–75% of image height or visual mass when its form permits, using a close or medium-close composition. Show the iconic shape in full enough to identify it; do not shrink it to explain a broad location. Favor a clear slightly elevated three-quarter view where suitable, not a distant establishing shot or overhead map.

SIMPLIFY THE ENVIRONMENT AGGRESSIVELY: Support the subject with only a few large readable environmental shapes — for example, a broad shoreline, a soft foliage mass, a simple path, a distant color-block building, or a quiet sky. They should establish context and mood, not become independent points of interest. Keep background geometry low-frequency, surfaces clean, and the number of small objects very limited. If a natural feature such as a tidal pool is itself the subject, make that contained feature a single strong, recognizable focal shape and simplify the surrounding rocks, water plants and tiny wildlife rather than filling every corner.

DETAIL BUDGET: Simplify construction, textures, railings, planks, leaves, stones, waves, interior fixtures and other repetitive details to large stylized forms. The finished result should feel approximately two to three times less busy than an elaborate environmental illustration of the same subject. Avoid intricate micro-props, thin repeating lines, dense decorative vegetation, heavily textured surfaces, realistic architecture, photographic lighting and full-scene atmosphere. Retain a few essential details that communicate the subject's identity.

DEPTH AND BACKGROUND: Allow some recognizable spatial layering, but maintain broad quiet areas and a clean silhouette. Use bright, controlled colors and soft dimensional lighting, consistent with Categories 1–3. Do not introduce cinematic fog, extreme depth of field, wide landscapes, heavy bloom, many small creatures or visual noise simply because the environment is more developed.

CONTENT: No readable letters, signage, product names, menus, numbers, logos or labels on buildings, carts, stalls, boards or equipment. Express the identity through shape and color instead. The environmental setting enhances the named collectible; it never replaces or visually dominates it.
~~~

## `category_5` — v3 (Prompt System v0.6)

~~~text
CATEGORY 5 — GOLDEN CARD, EXPRESSIVE CHARACTER MOMENT.

Create a golden-card-style illustration centered on one or two characters in a clear emotional, ironic, humorous, dramatic, or celebratory moment connected to the collection and set theme. The focus is expressive storytelling through character acting, not environmental detail.

Use exaggerated, appealing, readable character design with clear facial expression, pose, and silhouette. Keep the characters large and close enough to read well at thumbnail size. In most cases the characters should dominate the composition and occupy roughly 60–80% of the image height or visual mass together.

The setting may support the story, but it must stay simplified and secondary: a soft stylized backdrop, a few scene cues, light props, or a restrained environment. Keep the story readable without turning the image into a cinematic film still.

Preserve thematic mood when useful — mystery, winter fun, surprise, tension, celebration, mischief, triumph — but express it in the same polished casual collectible style. Prefer bright or controlled appealing color palettes, readable acting, and clear staging over dark realism, heavy grime, dense scenery, or moody cinematic effects.

No more than two characters unless the existing product rules explicitly allow otherwise.
~~~

## `image_closing` — v5 (Prompt System v0.8)

~~~text
FINAL ARTWORK COMPLIANCE CHECK

Generate ONE finished square collectible illustration only, using the active Category 1/2/3/4/5 composition module and the shared visual style. Do not blend background rules from multiple categories.

For Categories 1–4, the named collectible or coherent collectible unit must be immediately recognizable and visually dominant, with a clean silhouette, rounded stylized geometry, controlled detail and the background complexity specified by that category. For Category 5/Golden, focus on the expressive character or interaction and preserve the existing Golden narrative and character-count rules; do not apply a blanket 'no scene' instruction to Golden artwork.

Verify that the physical support, if any, matches the active category: none for Category 1; clearly differentiated horizontal and vertical planes for Category 2; context-appropriate physical surface and minimal surroundings for Category 3; simplified recognizable environment with a dominant subject for Category 4. Do not accidentally create Category 4 scenery for Category 3 or turn Category 2 into an environment.

Remove any readable text, letters, numerals, logos, menus, signage, product labels, watermarks, UI, card frames, borders, stars, rarity badges, caption boxes and unnecessary secondary clutter. Keep the image bright, readable and cohesive at thumbnail scale. Output the illustration itself, not an explanation or a card mockup.
~~~
