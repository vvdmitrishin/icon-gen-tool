## `system_style` — v4 (Prompt System v0.7)

~~~text
STYLE CORE — CASUAL MOBILE COLLECTIBLE ART.

Create ORIGINAL square collectible artwork in a polished casual-mobile-game style. The priority is STYLE FIDELITY OVER CREATIVE REINTERPRETATION.

If reference images are attached, analyze them first and treat them as the primary and authoritative visual specification. Match their visual language as closely as possible without copying the exact object, full composition, or any card UI. The result should look like it was created by the same art team for the same collectible collection.

Use a stylized, dimensional 3D-cartoon look with clean polished rendering, chunky simplified geometry, rounded forms, gentle bevels, clear silhouettes, and carefully controlled details. Materials should feel appealing and readable, with smooth shading, soft ambient occlusion, restrained highlights, and enough variation to distinguish wood, fabric, metal, glass, rubber, foliage, snow, stone, and similar surfaces without turning realistic or noisy.

Favor bright, saturated, attractive color palettes with strong separation between the main subject and the background. Use soft directional lighting that reveals volume and keeps forms readable. The image should feel friendly, intentional, and game-ready rather than cinematic, painterly, gritty, or photographic.

COLLECTIBLE SUBJECT FIRST. Show ONE clearly recognizable main collectible subject that is visually dominant: usually about 60–75% of the image's visual importance, or more when the category asks for it. It must read immediately at thumbnail size, with a clean silhouette and breathing room; never small, distant, or swallowed by the setting. The background and environment only support the subject and never compete with it: keep them simplified, stylized and low-noise, built from a few large shapes with limited secondary props. Avoid multiple equally important elements unless the concept explicitly requires a pair or group. If the subject is place-like or setting-like, choose its one iconic focal element and make that element dominate, so the result is a collectible illustration and not a scene.

Set and variant text may contain story or mood cues such as detective, winter, mystery, celebration, adventure, or nostalgia. Preserve those thematic cues, but express them inside this collectible art style. Do not let mood words push the image toward film stills, photorealism, dark cinematic grading, realistic skin, heavy texture, dramatic fog, bloom, gritty weathering, clutter, or overly detailed scenery.

Avoid: wide scenic or landscape-first compositions, top-down environment shots, location art, busy storytelling scenes, photorealism, generic stock-product rendering, flat vector graphics, sketchiness, micro-detail, realistic pores, dirt, scratches, heavy bokeh, excessive particles, cluttered props, and any readable text in the artwork: no words, letters, numbers, signs, signage, menus, labels, logos, typography, or decorative wording. Also no watermarks, card frames, badges, or visible UI.
~~~

## `category_1` — v3 (Prompt System v0.6)

~~~text
CATEGORY 1 — SINGLE COLLECTIBLE OBJECT, FLOATING.

Show one large, clearly recognizable collectible object as the only subject. It should float cleanly in space with no supporting surface and no environment. The object is the entire point of the image.

Use a simple but designed background: a bright gradient, soft color field, subtle concentric circles, restrained radial rays, or a similarly clean graphic treatment. The background should feel intentional and playful, not empty, but it must remain secondary to the object.

The object should usually occupy about 70–85% of the image height or visual mass. Use a clean readable angle, often a slightly elevated three-quarter view when appropriate. Keep the silhouette bold and easy to read.

Do not introduce a table, floor, shelf, room, landscape, or contextual props unless the subject absolutely requires one tiny attached component for recognition. Do not turn the background into a scene. No clutter.
~~~

## `category_2` — v3 (Prompt System v0.6)

~~~text
CATEGORY 2 — SINGLE COLLECTIBLE OBJECT ON ONE SIMPLE SUPPORT.

Show one large, clearly recognizable collectible object resting convincingly on one simple support surface. Suitable supports include a smooth tabletop, plain board, broad stone, simple mat, snow shelf, sand shelf, or another uncomplicated horizontal base that suits the subject.

The support exists only to ground the object. Include a soft contact shadow. Use a plain, saturated, or gently graded background, optionally with a subtle graphic motif, but do not build a full room or landscape.

The main object must remain the dominant focus and should usually occupy about 65–80% of the image height or visual mass. The support should be visually simple in shape and texture.

Do not add shelves, furniture, distant scenery, multiple props, decorative collections, or interior/exterior storytelling. Category 2 is richer than Category 1 only because the object is grounded on one simple support.
~~~

## `category_3` — v4 (Prompt System v0.7)

~~~text
CATEGORY 3 — SINGLE COLLECTIBLE OBJECT WITH IMMEDIATE CONTEXT.

Show one clearly recognizable collectible object as the main subject, placed on a contextual surface or in a lightly developed immediate setting that helps explain what it is. The main subject still dominates the image.

Use one believable support or immediate context layer such as a wooden deck, grass patch, workbench, picnic table, stone slab, rug, dock, counter, or similar surface that fits the subject. Behind it, allow a soft, simplified, low-detail background or a lightly developed stylized setting. The background should support recognition but remain secondary.

The main object should usually occupy about 60–75% of the image height or visual mass. The context should improve clarity, not compete for attention. Keep large shapes simple and readable, with only a few limited secondary props.

If the subject is a stand, kiosk, shelter, pool, or other small place-like subject, pick its single most iconic element and let it dominate the card; keep the surroundings to a few large simple shapes, as a close, centered collectible view and not a wide shot of the location.

Do not escalate into a full complex room, deep landscape, or busy narrative scene. Avoid many secondary objects, and show no signs, menus, labels, or readable writing on the object or the surfaces. Category 3 should feel richer than Category 2 through context, not through noise.
~~~

## `category_4` — v4 (Prompt System v0.7)

~~~text
CATEGORY 4 — MAIN COLLECTIBLE IN A RECOGNIZABLE STYLIZED ENVIRONMENT.

Show one clearly recognizable main collectible subject inside a recognizable but simplified environment. The environment may be indoor or outdoor, but it must stay stylized, low-noise, and subordinate to the main subject.

The main subject must still be visually dominant and immediately identifiable. In most cases it should remain large, usually around 60–75% of the image height or visual mass. The background can contain a few large supporting shapes such as walls, foliage masses, paths, hills, planks, windows, huts, fences, or simple architectural forms, with only a few limited secondary props, never a busy realistic scene.

If the planned subject is object-like, keep that object clearly dominant. If the planned subject is more place-like or setting-like, identify the most iconic focal element from the description and make that focal element dominate the composition so the card still reads as a collectible illustration rather than a landscape. Frame it close, centered and heroic, not as a wide establishing shot or a top-down view.

Use a simplified stylized environment with modest depth and a clear mood, while keeping materials, lighting, and detail density under control. The scene should help the subject, not overpower it.

Avoid tiny distant subjects, sprawling scenery, wide scenic or landscape-first compositions, cluttered props, multiple equally important elements, realistic environmental noise, deep cinematic atmosphere, and anything that makes the image read as a location illustration instead of a collectible card artwork. No signs, menus, labels, or any readable writing anywhere in the scene.
~~~

## `category_5` — v3 (Prompt System v0.6)

~~~text
CATEGORY 5 — GOLDEN CARD, EXPRESSIVE CHARACTER MOMENT.

Create a golden-card-style illustration centered on one or two characters in a clear emotional, ironic, humorous, dramatic, or celebratory moment connected to the collection and set theme. The focus is expressive storytelling through character acting, not environmental detail.

Use exaggerated, appealing, readable character design with clear facial expression, pose, and silhouette. Keep the characters large and close enough to read well at thumbnail size. In most cases the characters should dominate the composition and occupy roughly 60–80% of the image height or visual mass together.

The setting may support the story, but it must stay simplified and secondary: a soft stylized backdrop, a few scene cues, light props, or a restrained environment. Keep the story readable without turning the image into a cinematic film still.

Preserve thematic mood when useful — mystery, winter fun, surprise, tension, celebration, mischief, triumph — but express it in the same polished casual collectible style. Prefer bright or controlled appealing color palettes, readable acting, and clear staging over dark realism, heavy grime, dense scenery, or moody cinematic effects.

No more than two characters unless the existing product rules explicitly allow otherwise.
~~~

## `image_closing` — v4 (Prompt System v0.7)

~~~text
FINAL CHECKS.

Generate original square artwork only.

The final image must look like a collectible illustration from one coherent casual mobile game collection. Prioritize thumbnail readability, ONE clearly dominant main subject, clean silhouette, controlled detail, a simple low-noise background, and strong subject-background separation. It must not read as a scene, a location, or a wide landscape.

Respect the requested category structure. Keep the category differences visible: Category 1 floating and graphic; Category 2 grounded on one simple support; Category 3 with immediate context; Category 4 with a recognizable stylized environment; Category 5 as an expressive character story moment.

If reference images are attached, stay faithful to their style language first.

No readable text of any kind in the artwork: no words, letters, numbers, signs, menus, labels, captions, or logos. Do not draw card frames, titles, stars, UI, or watermarks. Do not output a contact sheet or multiple panels. Produce one final artwork image for the card.
~~~
